How we got here

From one question to a working system

We followed every purchase in VZI's own records, from the need on site to the shelf, and let the data show where AI earns its place.

The questionA Scope of Work: diagnose VZI's SAP and Ariba processes, find where AI creates value, and give a phased plan. Nine focus areas.
What we found23 problems, spread across all nine areas, not sitting in one department. Each one is an AI initiative, grouped into 8 value levers.
We split them into two phasesStarting where the data was already strong enough to trust. Phase 1, spares and stock, now. Phase 2, buying and sourcing, to July 2027.

Phase 1 now, Phase 2 next

Phase 1 puts the right parts on the shelf. Phase 2 changes how VZI buys them.

Phase 1 · built, in final testingSpares and stock
  • Stock levels that keep up, recommended all year from real usage, lead time and price.
  • Every repair in view, so a repaired part is used before a new one is bought.
  • Every part that could become idle, tracked, with a next step: use it, move it or return it.
  • Requests guided by real use, shown as the request is raised.
  • Inside SAP and Ariba, with no new licences. A person approves every recommendation.
Who gainsOperationsBuyers and controllersFinanceChief Commercial Officer
Phase 2 · to July 2027The way VZI buys
  • Find cheaper parts and suppliers
  • Check prices against the market
  • Buy faster
  • Stop buying the same thing twice
  • Track every saving
  • Check invoices automatically
  • Sell idle stock back to suppliers

Phase 1 and Phase 2 together are worth an indicative ZAR 15.2M to 35M a year across eight value levers. Every value is baselined against VZI's own SAP records before build, and tracked monthly after it.
